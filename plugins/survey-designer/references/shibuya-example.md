# Shibuya worked example, not default project settings

Use the supplied Survey Prompt.md and Google Forms Script Prompt.md, rather than the older version earlier in the conversation. Source: presentation slides 52–58 and the current prompts supplied October 18, 2026.

Research question: How is perceived tourist crowding in central Tokyo related to where greater Tokyo residents go for dining, shopping and leisure?

H1: Residents perceiving more tourist crowding in Shibuya went out there less often in the past three months. H2: Residents aged 18–34 went out there less often in that period than older residents. H3: Among people who avoid Shibuya, tourist crowding is the most common reason compared with cost, nightlife rules, shops and life changes. H4: Among avoiders, Saitama residents are more likely than 23-ward residents to go out in Omiya instead.

Population: Japanese adults 18+ living in Tokyo's 23 wards or Saitama Prefecture; do not expand eligibility to people who merely work/study there. Recruitment: social-media convenience sample; obtain usable younger and older groups, and at least 30 actual avoiders in each residence group. Do not infer avoiders from counts.

Ask counts only for the past three months. Use one categorical comparison with two years ago (more/about the same/less), not exact September 2019 counts. Include an early neutral yes/no avoidance question to measure the group and support later routing. Distinguish zero outings, avoidance and reduced visits. Clarify whether tourist means overseas visitors or domestic plus overseas; do not equate appearance/language with verified visitor identity.

Define Shibuya and going out once. Behavior and unprompted reasons precede prompted causes. Measure general crowd aversion as a control and evaluate cost, the applicable drinking restriction, shopping alternatives and life changes neutrally. Do not assert Omiya is cheaper or closer for every resident. Keep reason alternatives comparably broad and define any grouping before comparing their frequency.

Output: natural Japanese questionnaire with English researcher translation, stable variables and numeric answer codes, variable roles, hypothesis coverage, required/optional items, routing, proposed scores and support/contradiction criteria. Collect actual consent details. At completion ask the output format. If Google Forms is selected, generate createForm(), preserve the Japanese version, route with single-choice responses, log edit/respondent links and state any remaining limitations.

Regression checks: no 2019 recall; residence-only eligibility; direct avoidance group; both H2 age groups; both conditional H4 region groups; no early output-format question; no unsupported count routing or silent optional-visible fallback.
