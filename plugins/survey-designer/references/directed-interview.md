# Directed interview

Ask adaptive questions, not a long intake form. Ask one consequential question per turn; two only for the same immediate decision. Skip already answered questions. Use natural language and a concrete example; maintain decisions across turns.

| Gap | Example directed question | Design decision |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome | “What behavior or view do you want to explain or compare?” | Establish the outcome. |
| Mode | “Do you have an expectation you want to test, or do you want to explore what people say?” | Hypothesis-led versus exploratory. |
| Hypothesis | “Which group do you expect to differ, and in which direction?” | Predictor/outcome/comparison. |
| Population | “Who exactly should be eligible—where, which ages, and what relevant experience?” | Inclusion/exclusion and relevance. |
| Construct | “By ‘avoid,’ do you mean choosing not to go, going less, or never having gone?” | Distinguish constructs. |
| Recall | “What recent period could people reasonably remember?” | Period and measurement. |
| Rivals | “What else could explain the same pattern?” | Competing explanations and controls. |
| Recruitment | “How will you reach people, and which groups need to be compared?” | Actual sampling and conditional counts. |
| Language/burden | “What language will respondents use, and how much time can they spend?” | Comprehension and burden. |
| Consent facts | “Who is conducting the study, who can see answers, and when will they be deleted?” | Actual information/consent; split across turns if needed. |
| Output | “Which output format would you like?” | Only after questionnaire completion. |

If the topic alone is given, help narrow it: “What do you want to find out about [topic]—for example, whether [factor] relates to [outcome], or why people [behavior]?” Clearly mark proposed expectations as suggestions, not established decisions.

If an existing complete questionnaire is supplied, audit it instead of restarting intake. If the user gives a detailed brief, draft after resolving only material gaps. Never keep asking questions just to complete a prescribed number of turns.

For conditional quotas, ask about reaching the actual groups. In the Shibuya example H4 requires avoiders in each region, not just people in each region, and H2 needs an older comparison group. Explain feasibility without promising quotas establish representativeness.

Collect actual consent facts: researcher/institution/course or sponsor, contact, neutral purpose, topics, identifiers, use, access, storage, deletion, eligibility, optionality, withdrawal, expected time, compensation and foreseeable risks. Do not invent an email, deletion date, storage policy or absence of risk. Leave clearly marked placeholders if deferred and label the draft provisional. Never promise that an individual anonymous record can be retrieved/deleted after submission if there is no linkage. Email collection off alone is not proof of complete anonymity.

Keep a compact working brief with settled facts, decisions and remaining gaps. Do not repeatedly recite it. Do not ask about output format/platform/destination early. If volunteered, remember it and proceed to that export after completion without asking again.

At completion show the instrument and concise researcher/pilot notes, then ask the format question. Do not add another approval gate. If the user requests revisions, revise and audit before export. Questions during this interview are substantive directed design questions; never ask the user to approve routine internal implementation choices.
