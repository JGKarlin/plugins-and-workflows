# Shibuya Leisure Survey Prompt

```text
Design a survey questionnaire that tests the research question and hypotheses below.

Research question:
How is perceived tourist crowding in central Tokyo related to where greater Tokyo residents go for dining, shopping and leisure?

Hypotheses:
- H1: Residents who perceive more tourist crowding in Shibuya went out there less often in the past 3 months.
- H2: Residents aged 18–34 went out in Shibuya less often in the past 3 months than older residents.
- H3: Among residents who avoid Shibuya, tourist crowding is the most common reason, ahead of cost, nightlife rules, shops or life changes.
- H4: Among residents who avoid Shibuya, those in Saitama are more likely than those in the 23 wards to go out in Omiya instead.

Respondents: Japanese adults (18+) who live in Tokyo's 23 wards or Saitama Prefecture.

Sample: via social media, until there are 30+ aged 18–34 (H2) and 30+ who avoid Shibuya each in Saitama and the 23 wards (H4).

Target population and sample:
- Write for this population; every question must be relevant to them.
- Screen out anyone outside this population.
- State who results describe (a convenience sample) and that one survey shows links, not causes.
- Identify Shibuya avoiders with one early yes/no question, not visit counts.

Question content and wording:
- Write for respondents in natural, everyday Japanese.
- One idea per question; no double-barreled items.
- Simple, brief, jargon-free language; clear instructions per type.
- Define key terms once, e.g., what counts as Shibuya and as going out.
- No leading, loaded or emotional wording, especially about tourists.
- Check for cultural and social bias; no answer should look more acceptable than another.
- Exact frequency scales, not vague words like “often” or “sometimes”.
- Specific places, periods and reasons, not broad categories.
- Balanced options over the full range; keep “no opinion” off the scale.
- Likert scales for attitudes, multiple choice for behaviour and facts, one or two open questions for reasons in respondents’ own words.

Order and length:
- Consent and screening first; sensitive questions later, never early.
- Ask about behaviour and reasons before any question that names a possible cause, such as tourists.
- Group questions by topic in a logical flow; demographics last.
- About 5 minutes; cut any question that does not test a hypothesis.

Variables:
- Dependent: Shibuya outings in the past 3 months and compared with two years ago, and where residents go instead.
- Independent: perceived tourist crowding (H1), age group (H2), where they live: 23 wards or Saitama Prefecture, with ward, city or town (H4).
- Rivals (H3): cost of going out; Shibuya’s street-drinking ban; Shibuya as just a place to shop, when closer, cheaper Omiya will do; life changes, such as moving or a new job.

Measurement:
- Several items per concept; never ask the research question directly.
- Ask counts for the past 3 months only; add one question comparing with two years ago (more, about the same, less).
- Name each variable as dependent, independent or control.
- Add controls for other explanations, such as disliking any crowd.
- List each possible reason as an equal option, in random order, plus “Other”.
- Answer groups must match the hypotheses, e.g., ages 18–34 and 35+.
- Demographics only if they relate to a hypothesis.

Return:
- The full questionnaire in Japanese, in order, with all answer options, and an English translation beside each for checking.
- For each question: a number, a short variable name, numeric answer codes, and the hypothesis and variable it measures.
- Which items combine into one score, and any to reverse code.
- Which questions are required or optional (sensitive ones optional), and the skip logic.
- For each hypothesis, which results would support it and which would not.
- What to check in a pre-test with a few people.

Before you answer, check every question against all of the rules above and revise any that break one.
```
