# Survey-design principles and operating rules

Designed by Jason G. Karlin for survey design in academic research and for research design modeling. Read this guide in both skills and use it to decide what to ask, how to operationalize concepts and how to review the instrument. Distinguish general principles from illustrative examples and flawed questions discussed as pitfalls. An example is not a validated scale. The survey and Google Forms prompts illustrate the worked example.

## Principles in practice

| Principle | Required action in design-survey | Required action in export-survey |
|---|---|---|
| Audiences can be defined by place, people, medium, content or time. Methods answer different questions. | Establish the population and unit of analysis; identify what self-report can answer and where interviews/observation are needed. | Preserve definitions and eligibility; do not imply a form objectively observes behavior or interpretation. |
| Qualiquant methods combine numerical and open accounts; exploratory work can build theory. | Establish study mode, allow legitimate exploration without forced hypotheses, and leave room for unexpected meanings. | Preserve open items and distinguish planned versus exploratory analysis in researcher notes. |
| A focused study connects particular items to hypotheses; expectations can be unsupported. | Map hypotheses to items and specify expected/contradictory patterns before data collection. | Preserve stable identifiers and the planned codebook. |
| Unexpected findings invite artifact checks and exploratory follow-up; avoid HARKing. | Check recruitment and measurement artifacts before explaining surprising results; label later hypotheses exploratory. | Version changes; never invent observed results or rewrite original expectations retrospectively. |
| Research question, hypotheses, then questionnaire; remove irrelevant and excessive items. | Give each item an objective, hypothesis, control, consent or screening purpose; operationalize instead of asking the research question verbatim. | Do not add/drop questions during conversion; keep hypotheses out of respondent titles. |
| Clear brief unbiased wording, one idea, precise frequency, specificity and balanced options. | Audit wording, recall, overlap, full substantive range, zero/none and social/cultural bias. | Preserve exact text, labels and specified randomization; verify imports. |
| Population, sampling and comparison groups determine inference. | Define eligibility, actual recruitment method and conditional group counts; assess feasibility. | Implement exclusions and document actual inference limits; form settings do not create representativeness. |
| Order can prime answers; group topics and place sensitive questions later. | Ask behavior/unprompted reasons before suggested causes and attitudes; put demographics last except necessary screening. | Create sections by topic, with supported navigation, not a page for every question. |
| Pilot before wide implementation; assess hypotheses against results. | Prepare comprehension, recall, routing, mobile and timing checks; allow inconclusive evidence. | Test actual respondent paths and do not call a structurally checked file a tested live form. |
| Separate dependent/independent/control variables; several related items may form one score. | Distinguish outcome, predictors and rivals; justify composites, direction and missingness. | Preserve scores/reverse coding in researcher documentation without silently changing labels. |
| The police-support item blends support with reform and puts no opinion at a false midpoint. | Separate constructs; distinguish neutral from no information. Never reuse this flawed item as good practice. | Keep unknown/refusal/NA outside the numerical continuum and out of averages. |
| Convenience sampling, social desirability, broad categories and causal overreach are pitfalls. | Identify who the sample describes, use comparable categories, and avoid causal conclusions. | Keep these limits in researcher notes and keep the respondent instrument neutral. |
| Shibuya example: outcomes, rivals, controls and a direct avoidance grouping question. | Define intentional avoidance independently of frequency/change; check actual avoiders in each region. | Route from the measured single-choice group, not an unsupported count calculation. |
| Consent, privacy, required flags and routing belong to implementation. | Ask actual consent facts, collect minimal information, and reconcile optionality promises. | Disable unwanted identity collection; test refusal/exclusion and honor required flags. |
| The prompt models a method; one questionnaire can support many formats with consistent codes. | Separate reusable principles from project settings; store a stable specification. | Generate each format/codebook from the same version; disclose unsupported behavior/manual work. |

## Measurement rules

Match item type to the task: categorical choices for facts and behavior, exact periods/counts or nonoverlapping intervals for frequency, appropriate ordered scales for attitudes, and one or two purposeful open questions by default. Do not ask age using agreement or duplicate it merely to have multiple items per concept.

Use several distinct items for a latent construct when justified. State any proposed composite, direction, minimum answered-item count, reverse coding and missingness rule. Do not add reverse wording automatically or average unrelated predictors because they share a scale. A design audit cannot establish empirical reliability or validity.

Keep zero/none distinct from neutral, no opinion, insufficient information, NA, refusal, routed skip and unanswered. Keep all missing states outside substantive numerical scores. Avoid exact long-term count recall merely to manufacture change; consider life-stage, relocation and exposure when historical comparison matters.

Measure plausible rival explanations rather than giving the preferred explanation all the attention. Use comparably broad main-reason categories, or predefine aggregation if rivals need subcategories. Keep the single main reason distinct from multiple contributing reasons. Never label self-reported reasons as established causal effects.

In the Shibuya example, do not assume Omiya is cheaper/closer for every person, that tourists must be foreign, or that a nonvisitor is intentionally avoiding the area. Ask neutral measures and define the tourist scope explicitly. Treat the hypotheses as expectations to test, including possible contradiction.

## Sampling qualifications

Calculator margins such as 95%/±5% for about 385 require sampling and estimation assumptions; do not apply them automatically to ordinary social-media convenience samples. Age/region quotas do not transform convenience recruitment into stratified random sampling.

Thirty per group is a practical target in the worked example, not a universal power calculation or representativeness guarantee. Count actual usable denominators after screening, routing and nonresponse. For H4 count avoiders in each residence group; for H2 include both younger and older respondents. Do not require 30 in every age-by-region intersection unless that is the planned comparison.

A one-time self-report survey supports associations and reported accounts, not causal direction. Attitudes may influence recollection and avoidance may limit direct exposure. Keep planned hypotheses separate from later exploratory hypotheses.

## Completion audit

Revise any failing item before declaring the questionnaire complete.

| Check | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Every substantive question has an objective/hypothesis/control purpose and applies to whoever sees it. |
| Content | One idea, precise definitions, consistent periods and no unsupported premise. |
| Wording | Everyday respondent language; neutral, brief, culturally appropriate; no answer looks morally preferred. |
| Answers | Full substantive range, no overlapping categories, precise frequency, meaningful zero, unknown/NA separate from scale. |
| Sequence | Consent/eligibility first, behavior and unprompted reasons before candidate causes, topics grouped, sensitive questions later. |
| Measurement | Correct variable roles, justified composites, score direction, missingness, comparable reason categories and correct subgroup denominators. |
| Routing | Valid targets, no cycles, unambiguous conditions, safe optional nonresponse paths, no hidden required fields, all screen-outs end. |
| Consent | Actual study facts, truthful access/storage/withdrawal/risk language and matching requiredness. |
| Burden | No duplication or unnecessary demographics; duration is a pilot target, not an untested claim. |
| Fidelity | All outputs preserve version, text, labels, IDs/codes, scoring, sections and routing. |

## Pre-test and researcher feedback

Ask a few target-population members to explain key terms and how they choose answers. Check recall feasibility and option fit. Exercise consent refusal, exclusions, zero use, unknown, NA, other text, optional nonresponse and every substantive branch. Time the actual survey and inspect it on a phone. Revise based on observed problems; do not invent pilot results.

Explain necessary choices briefly to the researcher. Keep explanations brief. Apply these principles to the next directed question and the instrument itself rather than appending a generic checklist.
